Sweet Revenge Never Smelled So Sharp


Flight QR 708

Shubhrangshu Roy

I was half way scissoring into the Atlantic, a good five hours on an eastbound flight out of DC when that sinking feeling drowned me in a sudden spasm of panic, creeping darkness shrouding my face soaking in nervous sweat.

I had spent the better part of the long hot summer day mowing the yard before flying out of Dulles.

Was the heat taking its toll, I asked myself. Was it anxiety of the unknown? Hours earlier, Zara wondered driving me down to departure if she would ever fly across the Pacific. "Have you looked at the atlas? It is one helluva half of earth. What if...?"

What if, I asked now, sinking, on a flight commanded by Pedro for a flashy Arabian flagship split longitudinally between PLUs and PLTs, us and them, for maximum dollar... on a day Space X's Elon Musk became history's first trillionaire tycoon.

And here I was, crammed into cattle class between a blonde and a brunette, jutting out like, well, the middle finger, not quite the sore thumb, yet, before I could press the panic button and call for help. As it had turned out, Ms Blonde was radiant Black, Ms Brunette, pale White, both in fancy hairpieces. She is lissome, cuddly and smiling. She is clumsy, distant and stern.

And I sat between the two and how, melting in my fifty shades of grey, fear darkening my choices, heading into total blackout.

Up there under the open sky, 12,000 metres above mid-ocean, a long, jagged spine - the mid-Atlantic ridge - showed up on my seatback map, running north to south, before my gaze spiralled into a black hole.

Flashes of shaven grass strewn across the yard, the drive down I-495 from home, Zara at the wheels, the Pacific, the hairdo to my left and right, Pedro in command.
Pedro!

Who exactly was the trigger?
Or what?
Was it the silly dry chicken tikka with the sillier drier sticky rice, in place of the inviting beef enchilada, for dinner, for third class you and I? Or, perhaps, the twin swigs of unsigned, unlabelled, unidentifiable red wine in plastic double shot cups... was that at work?

I faintly loosened my shoestrings to feel my feet going numb, unbuttoned my T, and let my mind float... adrift, desperately seeking help deep inside where there was none ... there was nobody to ask, nobody to scream to, no airstrip in sight for an emergency landing, within blinking distance. The French coast on the horizon, nearest, still three hours too far away. My heart skipped a beat..

It was the calm before the storm. Literally.

With my head still reeling in the darkness within, our plane suddenly went down... with a thud, in a flash, in the engulfing darkness outside. We had plunged 2,000 metres on my seatback map... in a second.

Our ship rocked, and rolled, and rattled... like a ricketty rickshaw speeding along an unmetalled track down a broken slope at breakneck speed.

"Fasten seatbelts, fasten seat belts, cabin crew to station," Pedro hollered on the public address system, with the urgency of his hair on fire, as a hundred horror-sticken pairs of eyes rolled to a collective sigh in the surround.

And our plane went tumbling down and lumbering up, up and down, down and up, rolling right to left, left to right. Pacific. Atlantic. What difference does it make... I heard someone ask Zaru deep inside my head.

How different is black from white, blonde from brunette, hair from grass, grass from weed, weed from acid? Acid!

I felt the acid roll in my bowel. Deep down there in the dark. The acid had triggered the dark. The dark had ignited the acid. In that stuffy, sweaty cattle class... between blonde and brunette.

And in that turmoil within and that turbulence outside... I exploded.

I belched!

A loud silence descended on my zone. Ms White in brunette to my right covered her mouth and turned her face to the aisle. Ms Black in blonde to my left turned to the window and raised the shade.

We were floating above a vast seamlessly deep pile of grey clouds below an endlessly luminous bright early morning sky... into a bottomless ocean of calm. All was quiet again, drowning my turmoil within and the turbulence outside in that burp.

It was still eight hours to landing.
I remembered Zara.
Pacific? Atlantic?
I was sailing into tomorrow.
Steady. Confident. In Light.
...
Our aeroplane descended on the tarmac, and taxied by the Gulf, within missile shot from Thundering Tehran. A gut-wrenching sulfurous noiseless wind drifted into my nostrils, assaulting my sense of left and right.

Right or Left?
Blonde or Brunette?

Sweet revenge never smelled so acrid.
